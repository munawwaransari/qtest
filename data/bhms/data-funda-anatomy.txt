Which nerve is the thickest nerve in the body?

[A] Femoral nerve
[B] Sciatic nerve
[C] Obtrutor nerve
[D] None of the above
B
~
The sciatic nerve is the thickest (and longest) nerve in the human body, originating in the lower back, running through the buttocks and down the back of each leg, and is a bundle of nerves that can reach up to 2 centimeters in width, connecting the spinal cord to the leg and foot. 
~

- 6
Foot drop is caused by injury to

[A] Peroneal verve
[B] Sciatic nerve
[C] Obtrutor nerve
[D] Tibial nerve
A
~
Foot drop (also called drop foot) happens when you can’t raise the front part of your foot due to weakness or paralysis of the muscles that lift it. It’s a symptom of several possible underlying conditions and can be temporary or long-term (chronic).

Foot drop has several possible causes, typically due to an issue with your nerves and/or muscles. The most common causes are lumbar radiculopathy and peroneal nerve injury.
~

- 8
Which of these is unpaired cartilages of larynx

[A] Arytenoid Cartilages
[B] Epiglottis
[C] Corniculate Cartilages
[D] Cuneiform Cartilages
B
~
The larynx (voice box) is supported by nine cartilages: three unpaired (thyroid, cricoid, epiglottis) and three paired (arytenoid, corniculate, cuneiform), forming its framework, which controls sound production by allowing muscles to move the vocal cords, with the thyroid forming the Adam's apple, the cricoid as a signet ring base, and the epiglottis acting as a lid.  

Unpaired Cartilages (Single):

Thyroid Cartilage: The largest, forming the front and sides (Adam's apple), made of hyaline cartilage, connects to the cricoid below and hyoid bone above. 

Cricoid Cartilage: A signet ring shape, forming the base of the larynx, connecting to the trachea below.

Epiglottis: A leaf-shaped elastic cartilage that covers the airway opening during swallowing. 
~
- 9
Which of these is paired cartilages of larynx

[A] Arytenoid Cartilages
[B] Epiglottis
[C] Cricoid Cartilage
[D] Thyroid Cartilage
A
~
The larynx (voice box) is supported by nine cartilages: three unpaired (thyroid, cricoid, epiglottis) and three paired (arytenoid, corniculate, cuneiform), forming its framework, which controls sound production by allowing muscles to move the vocal cords, with the thyroid forming the Adam's apple, the cricoid as a signet ring base, and the epiglottis acting as a lid.  

Paired Cartilages (Two of each):

Arytenoid Cartilages: Pyramidal, sitting atop the cricoid, with processes for vocal ligament and muscle attachment. 

Corniculate Cartilages: Small nodules atop the arytenoids, part of the aryepiglottic folds. 

Cuneiform Cartilages: Club-shaped elastic cartilages within the aryepiglottic folds, providing support. 
~
