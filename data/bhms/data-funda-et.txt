Consider the following statement:

Rapid transmission of impulses through purkinje fibres are due to

  I The purkinje fibres are large in size than other conducting fibres of heart 
 II Extremely high level of permeability of the gap junctions with purkinje fibres
III Purkinje fibres have a very few myofibrils
 IV Purkinje fibres have more fibrous tissues

Choose the most appropriate answer:
[A] I, II and III only
[B] I and IV only
[C] I and III only
[D] II and IV only
A
~
Purkinje fibers are specialized, electrically excitable cardiac muscle fibers that transmit electrical impulses to the ventricles, ensuring they contract in a coordinated manner. They are the terminal part of the heart's electrical conduction system, spreading out through the inner walls of the ventricles to rapidly and efficiently trigger the muscle contractions needed to pump blood from the heart.

The statement that Purkinje fibres have more fibrous tissues is incorrect in terms of cellular composition. Purkinje fibres are specialized muscle fibres (cells), not fibrous connective tissues.

The sensory modality which does not reach the thalamus directly is

[A] Proprioception
[B] Gustation
[C] Nociception
[D] Olfaction
D
~
The sensory modality that does not reach the thalamus directly is smell (olfaction); it's the only sense that bypasses the thalamus's relay, projecting directly from the olfactory bulb to the primary olfactory cortex and limbic system for direct emotional and memory links, unlike vision, hearing, taste, and touch which all have key thalamic relays.

- 5
The commonest form of obstetric palsy encountered in puerperium is:

[A] Erb's palsy
[B] Torticollis
[C] Foot drop
[D] Carpel tunnel syndrome
C
~
Erb's palsy is a common birth injury affecting the infant, while carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS) is a common maternal condition that can occur during pregnancy and persist into the puerperium (postpartum period).

Foot drop, though less common overall than LFCN, is a serious form of obstetric palsy, typically involving the L5 nerve root or common peroneal nerve, leading to difficulty lifting the front part of the foot (dorsiflexion). 

Key Points on Foot Drop in Puerperium:
Cause: Compression of the lumbosacral nerve roots (especially L5) or the common peroneal nerve by the baby's head, maternal positioning (lithotomy), or forceps during delivery.

Symptoms: Inability to lift the foot (foot drop), a slapping gait, weakness in the anterior compartment muscles, numbness/tingling on the outer leg and foot.

One unit of packed RBC raises haemoglobin by ______________ gm/dl

[A] 0.5
[B] 1
[C] 1.5
[D] 2
B
~
The generally accepted estimate is a ~10 g/L (1 g/dL) rise in Hb.

One unit of packed red blood cells (PRBCs) typically raises a non-bleeding adult's hemoglobin (Hb) level by approximately 10 g/L (or 1 g/dL) and hematocrit by about 3%, though this can vary slightly by patient factors like body mass and pre-transfusion Hb. 

Factors Influencing Increase: 
The actual rise depends on the patient's weight, gender, and pre-transfusion Hb level, with lower starting Hb potentially leading to a greater relative increase.

- 9
Who amongst the following propogated the blood-letting method?

[A] Galen
[B] Hippocrates
[C] Hahnemann
[D] Brousseau
D
~
Bloodletting as a practice originated in ancient Egypt and was established in ancient Greece, where Hippocrates and his followers integrated it into their medical theory. The French physician François Broussais was a major proponent who popularized the use of leeches for bloodletting in the 19th century, well after the time of Hippocrates. 

Bloodletting is not a method used in homeopathy; in fact, the founder of homeopathy, Samuel Hahnemann, was a strong opponent of the practice of bloodletting. Homeopathy is a distinct system of alternative medicine based on the principle of "like cures like" and the use of highly diluted substances, a direct reaction against the aggressive and often harmful medical practices of his time, such as bloodletting. 
~

The taste sensor from the posterior one third of the tongue is transmitted through

[A] Trigeminal nerve
[B] Glassopharyngeal nerve
[C] Facial nerve
[D] Hypoglossal nerve
B
~
The sensory pathway for taste on the tongue is divided among three cranial nerves: 

Facial nerve (CN VII): 
Carries taste information from the anterior two-thirds of the tongue (via the chorda tympani branch).

Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX): 
Carries taste information from the posterior one-third of the tongue.

Vagus nerve (CN X): 
Carries taste information from the epiglottis and lower pharynx (via the superior laryngeal nerve branch).

A 65 year old male alcoholic presents with 2 months history of swelling of the abdomen with shifting dullness and ankle oedema, shortness of breath on exertion. On examination, there are no signs of chronic liver disease. His pulse is 112/min with atrial fibrillation, apex beat is displaced downwards and laterally.

What is the most likely diagnosis?

[A] Deep vein thrombosis
[B] Pericarditis
[C] Nephrotic Syndrome
[D] Cardiomyopathy
D
~
The patient's clinical presentation is consistent with alcoholic cardiomyopathy (ACM), a type of dilated cardiomyopathy caused by long-term, heavy alcohol consumption. The standard and most crucial treatment is complete abstinence from alcohol, combined with conventional medical therapies for heart failure and atrial fibrillation.

Type I Diabetes Mellitus is associated with all of the following except:

[A] Insulitis
[B] Beta cell mass reduced
[C] Amyloidosis change
[D] Beta cell degranulation
C
~
Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus is an autoimmune disorder resulting in the destruction of insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas. In homeopathy, as per clinical observations reported by practitioners, it is associated with a variety of symptoms and comorbidities.

In conventional medicine, Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (T1DM) is not systematically associated with a specific type of systemic amyloidosis. However, a localized form called localized insulin-derived amyloidosis (LIDA) can occur as a rare complication of long-term subcutaneous insulin injections.
