- 2
Formulation of cardinal principles of homoeopathy is based on

[A] Inductive logic
[B] deductive logic
[C] Syllogism
[D] None of the above
D
~
The formulation of the cardinal principles of homoeopathy is primarily based on inductive logic and empirical observation/experimentation, not syllogism.

The formulation of the cardinal principles of homoeopathy is based on the natural law of healing, "Similia Similibus Curantur" (like cures like), and extensive experimentation and observations made by its founder, Dr. Samuel Hahnemann. These principles are primarily detailed in his seminal work, the Organon of Medicine.

- 3
Which law is described in this statement:

The central logical premise is that a disease can be cured by a substance that produces similar symptoms in a healthy person.

[A] The Single Remedy
[B] The Law of the Minimum Dose
[C] The Law of Similars
[D] Individualization
B
~
The Law of Similars ("like cures like"): This central principle states that a substance which causes symptoms in a healthy person can be used in a highly diluted form to treat similar symptoms in a sick person.

- 9
The following statement refers to which core principle of the Law of Cure

The artificial medicinal disease introduced into the body must be stronger than the natural disease it is intended to cure

[A] Dynamic Affections
[B] Symptom Similarity
[C] Superior Strength
[D] Difference in Kind
[E] Permanent Extinction
C
~
Superior Strength:
The artificial medicinal disease introduced into the body must be stronger than the natural disease it is intended to cure

That statement describes the core concept behind homeopathy, as formulated by Samuel Hahnemann in the early 19th century. The principle, known as the "law of similars" (Latin: similia similibus curentur, or "let likes be cured by likes"), posits that a substance that causes symptoms in a healthy person can be used in highly diluted form to treat similar symptoms in a sick person.
